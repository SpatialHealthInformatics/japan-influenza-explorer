Abstracts

Japanese Society for Pediatric Infectious Diseases · Yokohama · October 2026

Near Real-Time EHR Surveillance of Pediatric Influenza in Japan Compared With National Sentinel Surveillance

Background: Timely pediatric influenza surveillance is important for monitoring seasonal activity and supporting public health and clinical decision-making. Japan's national influenza surveillance system relies on sentinel reporting, which may involve reporting delays. Electronic health record (EHR) data offer an opportunity for near real-time surveillance. We evaluated whether pediatric influenza trends from TriNetX Japan were consistent with national sentinel surveillance trends.

Methods: We conducted a retrospective analysis of pediatric patients (under 18 years of age) with laboratory-confirmed influenza (PCR or antigen) identified through TriNetX Japan during the 2025–2026 influenza season. Weekly case counts were examined and compared with national sentinel surveillance trends.

Results: In this preliminary analysis, influenza activity increased in early October 2025 (around epidemiologic week 40), rising from fewer than 10 cases per week in late September to more than 60 by late October. The timing was broadly consistent with national sentinel surveillance reports.

Conclusions: Near real-time EHR data produced pediatric influenza trends broadly consistent with national sentinel surveillance. EHR-based surveillance may complement existing programs by providing timely access to laboratory-confirmed cases. Limitations include limited facility representation within TriNetX, possible differences between TriNetX and national healthcare-seeking populations, and TriNetX showing weekly counts of 1 to 10 as 10, which limits detection of early low-level activity.

Japan Epidemiological Association

Automated Intelligent Decision Support Framework for Influenza Surveillance in Japan

Background: Japan's national sentinel surveillance provides weekly influenza data for all 47 prefectures, published openly by JIHS with a 10-year history. Automating the steps after publication, from download to alert, could complement this system by turning each weekly release into timely action.

Objective: To evaluate a prototype of an automated intelligent decision support framework that turns published surveillance data into alerts, a first step toward interoperable surveillance in Japan.

Methods: The prototype has five functions: retrieval, detection, reporting, distribution, and notification. A scheduled R script retrieves the weekly IDWR files from the Japan Institute for Health Security, which contain weekly influenza reports per sentinel site. It runs the EARS C2 method (7-week baseline, 2-week guard band, alert at 3 SD, minimum SD 0.1) and writes an HTML report for people and a JSON summary for machines. The files go to SharePoint via the Microsoft Graph API, and Power Automate reads the JSON and sends an email alert. We evaluated timeliness by comparing the first C2 alert in each season from 2016/17 to 2025/26 with the week reports first exceeded 1.0 per sentinel site, Japan's epidemic threshold.

Results: In seven seasons with a distinct off-season and a threshold crossing, the first alert came before the threshold each time, by a median of 2 weeks (range 1 to 4). No alerts occurred in 2020/21 or 2021/22, when influenza was absent. In 2023/24, reports stayed above 1.0 through the summer, so no crossing occurred.

Discussion: Each function can be replaced without rebuilding the rest. The retrieval layer supports file-based or API-based inputs, allowing additional data sources to be integrated without changing the downstream workflow. The machine-readable summary makes the last step automatic. Limitations are the April 2025 sentinel site change and IDWR's publication lag.

Conclusion: A modular prototype can turn Japan's published surveillance data into timely alerts and a base for interoperability.
